# SUI design laws

These laws govern every SUI component and the kitchen sink that shows them. They come from two sheets: the [Smyklot panel design system](https://github.com/smykla-skalski/smyklot/blob/main/design-system/smyklot-panel/MASTER.md) (`S-n`) and the rules of a companion documentation site (`K-n`). The palette's grounds, lines, inks, accent and status colors come from a companion operations console. When the sheets disagree, Smyklot wins and the companion sheet fills what Smyklot does not say. Where the Smyklot sheet and its tested `tokens.css` disagree, SUI follows the tested code.

A sheet rule that goes against an accessibility or usability standard does not stand. Where a law departs from its sheet for that reason, it names the standard: WCAG 2.2, the WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices (APG), the Apple and Material platform guidelines, GOV.UK, or published research.

Each law names how it is held:

- `audit` - the kitchen sink measures it in the rendered page (`src/routes/audit/`), and the browser suite (`tests/browser/*.test.mjs`) fails on any finding at 320, 375, 768, 1440 and 2560px (an ultra-wide screen, or 50% zoom) in both themes, and checks the laws that need a keyboard, a media emulation, a right-to-left page or two renders
- `source` - `tests/laws.test.mjs` reads the component sources and the palette
- `review` - a person checks it, because no measurement decides it

A law that says "every" means every component, including the ones added after it was written. When a new component cannot meet a law, change the law here first.

## Palette and color

- **L-1** The palette (`src/lib/styles.css`) is the only place a color is written. Component styles read `var(--sui-*)` and never a hex, `rgb()`, `hsl()` or named color. A shade derived from a color the reader picked, with relative color syntax (`oklch(from var(…) …)`), is not a written color. `S-7` `source`
- **L-2** Every token a component reads is declared, and every declared token is read, directly or through another token. A scale is declared whole even when one step is unused. `S-8 S-9` `source`
- **L-3** Components read semantic tokens, never `--sui-primitive-*`. `K-1` `source`
- **L-4** A token derived with `var()` is restated in every palette block, so a dark subtree never keeps a light alias. A page that chooses no theme follows the reader's system preference to the same dark block. `S-10` `source`
- **L-5** Text holds 4.5:1 against the ground it is painted on, and large text (24px, or 18.66px at 700) holds 3:1, in both themes. Text also holds an APCA lightness contrast (APCA-W3) of Lc 60, or Lc 45 for large text, and the body ink holds Lc 75 on every surface, since WCAG 2 overrates a light ink on a dark ground and APCA reads it as it is seen. On dark, the quiet inks stay easy to read: muted ink holds 8:1 on the canvas and secondary ink 10:1. `S-11 S-212` `audit`
- **L-6** The focus ring holds 3:1 on the canvas, surfaces, control fills and input fills, and on a current row's plate over any of them. A control whose ring is the control itself (switch track, check box, choice dot) uses `--sui-border-strong`, which holds 3:1. `S-18 S-20` `audit`
- **L-7** Ink on a tone fill comes from its partner token (`--sui-on-brand-action`, `--sui-on-danger`, `--sui-on-decision-accent`), never a literal white. `S-12` `source`
- **L-8** Structural hairlines stay subtle: `--sui-border-subtle`, which frames a surface, is between 1.2:1 and 2:1 on `--sui-surface-base`, and `--sui-border-divider`, which separates rows, heads, feet and tab lines inside a surface, is between 1.1:1 and 1.5:1. `S-13` `audit`
- **L-9** A button's words identify it, so its boundary may stay quiet: `--sui-control-border` on `--sui-control-bg` is between 1.5:1 and 2.25:1, and on the inset ground, `--sui-surface-inset`, between 1.4:1 and 2.25:1. A copy control in a code block's own seat rests on the block's hairline (`--sui-border-subtle`), since the block's frame already holds it and a framed button down each block of a column reads as a column of buttons, and it firms to a button's hover edge on hover. A quiet action on a save bar's gold frame stands on the neutral raised ground, not the gold plate its unsaved edge is tuned for, so it leans toward the frame's edge until it holds 3:1 on that ground in both themes, seven tenths of the way in both, 3.1:1 on light and 3.6:1 on dark, where a weaker mix reads olive beside the bright gold fill, still under the frame's. A field is a quiet frame with a baseline: the frame (`--sui-field-frame`, a button's border on light, so a field and a button share one edge and the top edge with its recess never reads heavier than the sides, and a step firmer on dark, never lighter than a button's) shapes it, and the baseline (`--sui-field-border`) is what shows that a field is there, so it holds 3:1 against every ground a field stands on and against the field's own fills, as the minimal input in the WCAG 1.4.11 examples does. An invalid field takes the danger color on all four sides, and a focused field keeps its frame under the ring of L-41. A select whose picker is open draws its baseline in the accent, two strokes deep, so the field that owns the open list reads at a glance. An invalid choice card draws the danger edge on its frame, and its empty dot stays neutral, so the frames and the error carry the alarm. An invalid check box or switch draws a 2px danger edge, so it reads on the accent fill when checked or on as well as when empty: inside the box, so an invalid box keeps the size of the others, and on a switch, whose thumb fills its track, a 1px edge doubled outward, which drops its double with focus, so the red ring stands its 2px clear of it as it does off a field. This applies to text fields, text areas, select triggers, a pair entry's chips and joined fields, where the joined frame draws the one ground and edge and its parts draw none of their own in any state. On hover a field takes the row veil and its edges step up, with a button's edge firming by the same 12 points of ink in every theme (`--sui-control-border-hover`), a field's frame firming further, to 36% black on light and 38% white on dark (`--sui-field-frame-hover`), so a field answers the pointer at a glance while its ground only just moves, and an invalid field or choice card hovers in its state's hue instead, a danger veil (`--sui-danger-hover-layer`) under its danger edge a step deeper (`--sui-danger-hover`), as Material's state layers take the error color, so the hover never hides the state. A field is sunk into its ground: one line just under its top edge, an inset a stroke deep with no blur (`--sui-field-recess`), is darker than its frame and its ground, as Primer's form controls draw their inset shadow and Polaris darkens a field's top edge. On light it stands a third to a half of a hover step (L-11) past the frame at rest, so the top edge reads no heavier than the sides, a whole step on hover, where it steps with the frame (`--sui-field-recess-hover`); on dark, where the frame is lighter than the field, it is a shade that deepens on hover as the frame firms. It stays under the ring and the danger edge, dims with a busy field, takes the unsaved family in a changed row (L-16) and goes with the baseline on a read-only or disabled field (L-40). It is the field's shape, not a state, so it never reads as a press, which adds the press inset and the sink (L-42). `S-14` `audit`
- **L-10** A control fill is never a focal surface: `--sui-control-bg` on `--sui-surface-base` stays under 1.5:1. A read-only field is a distinct inset: `--sui-surface-inset` on `--sui-surface-base` is between 1.05:1 and 1.3:1. A field that is off or only reads sits in the field well (`--sui-field-well`), between 1.05:1 and 1.3:1 from the canvas, the surface and the raised ground: the inset ground on light, and on dark, where the inset ground stands within a level of the raised one, a 7% veil of white, so the well reads on every ground a field stands on and a field that is off never stands out as the lightest block among the dark fields. A field that is off takes a quieter tint, 75% of the well (`--sui-field-disabled`), so it draws less of the eye than a field to type in, and a read-only one 55%, which its frame tells apart, and both stay between 1.05:1 and 1.3:1 from every ground. A field that is off fills its tint and drops its frame, so it reads as flat and out of use. A field that only reads keeps a quiet frame over a lighter tint, 16% on light and 20% on dark (`--sui-field-readonly-frame`), firmer than a card's keyline so its value keeps a contour and lighter than an editable field's frame, 55% of the well in both themes (`--sui-field-readonly`), so the full well is left to a field that is off, at least 0.6 past a hovered field's tint so a field under the pointer never looks locked, so a value to read or copy never looks like a field that is off, while its quiet frame and missing recess keep it from looking like one to type in. A field that a saving setting row holds wears the read-only paint with its words at full strength, so it never looks open to typing while its value saves. A fill never marks a field alone. The edge of L-9 does that. `S-15 S-16` `audit`
- **L-11** Hover is one state change and press is two, in one direction. Measured as CIEDE2000 from the ground: on a light ground hover is 4.4 to 5.4 and press 7.2 to 8.3, on a dark ground hover is 5.3 to 6.7 and press 9 to 10.5. Hover and press are alpha veils over the ground, never flat fills. A hover or highlight never takes contrast away: on dark, where the veil lightens the ground, the words on it rise to `--sui-hover-ink`, the bright ink, and read at or above their rest Lc; on light the veil darkens the ground and the words keep the rest ink, which is already the darkest. A stop item keeps its danger ink. The row a floating list puts under the pointer or the keyboard (a select's picker, a combobox list, a menu, an addition picker) takes a veil of the accent half a step past hover, between the hover and press steps, since it holds the keyboard's place (`--sui-list-highlight`), and the press step when pressed (`--sui-list-pressed`), as Radix's menus light the item a reader acts on, so it reads at a glance on the popover ground, while a check marks what is chosen. A static copy's lit row wears the focus ring as a combobox's lit row does. `S-27 S-30` `audit`
- **L-12** A row's hover step is about half a control's: 2 to 3 on light, 4.5 to 5.5 on dark. A field, which also firms its frame on hover, only just moves its ground, 0.7 to 1 on light and 1.5 to 1.9 on dark, where a firmer light frame reads less on a dark ground, so the frame carries the hover and a hovered field stays clear of the read-only tint (L-10). A choice card is framed as a field is and hovers as one, with the field's hover ground (`--sui-field-hover`) under its firmer edge, so a hovered card never looks sunk or outweighs the chosen one, and a chosen card, which a press would not change, does not answer. Its dot draws no ground of its own, so the inside of the dot takes the card's ground in every state and never stands as a white disc on a hovered, pressed, chosen or disabled card. `S-31` `audit`
- **L-13** The primary button walks one way along its ramp: rest, hover and press move lightness in one direction, and `--sui-on-brand-action` holds 4.5:1 on all three. Destructive hover deepens on light and brightens on dark, its fill leaning 18% toward the dark danger ink, a step the eye finds that keeps its red and its white words at 4.5:1. `--sui-brand-action` is one color in both themes, so the accent reads the same on either ground, and so is the stop fill `--sui-danger-fill`, a deep red under white words, while `--sui-danger` stays the danger ink and edge of each theme. `S-19 S-29` `audit`
- **L-14** The accent is one ocean blue that fills the same in both themes with white ink, and never a destructive color. Its text shade is a step deeper on light and a light blue on dark, and links, information and a sorted column's arrow wear it. Success is green, caution is amber, danger is muted rose. A role wears violet (`--sui-role-tint`, `--sui-role-ink`), never an outcome tint. Each hue has an ink for text and a quiet tint for grounds in each theme, and the ink holds 4.5:1 on its tint (L-5). Lines are black or white veils, so one line reads the same on every ground. Chart series have their own palette (L-66) and never wear these. `S-3 S-4 S-21` `review`
- **L-15** Color never carries state alone. Every state color comes with a word, or with an icon that has a text name. An `aria-hidden` icon tells a screen reader nothing, so the state needs words for it (WCAG 1.3.1). The budget is roughly 85% neutral, 10% accent and at most 5% semantic color. `S-5 S-6` `review`
- **L-16** Unsaved work is a caution, so it wears the caution hue: `--sui-decision-accent` is a gold fill beside the warning amber, with its own dark ink, and never stands as text, where the unsaved ink does. The gold `--sui-unsaved-*` surfaces and inks belong only to unsaved state and the action that resolves it, and they always come with their words or mark. A pane shows unsaved work in two places only: its save bar and the changed row's mark, never in a toast. Inside a changed row every edge, field border and hover or press veil takes the unsaved family too, so no neutral line or veil sits on the gold plate, while an on-state fill, a checked box, an on switch or a chosen segment, keeps the accent with its own edge, so a control has one on colour on every row of a page and the plate, its edges and the word carry the change. A save bar is its pane's form action, so at rest it stands 8px past the gap its content keeps, 24px past the last field on the 16px rhythm, as a page form's action does (L-35), and reads as the fields' close, not one more of them, while a bar with nothing above it starts where the content does. While a save or a reload runs, the save bar's edge and buttons dim together, and its copy, the live status the reader reads, keeps its ink, as a busy button's words keep theirs. A save refused because the work changed elsewhere offers Reload alone, as the decision, since saving again would be refused too, and a reload offers Undo, which puts the reader's changes back over the latest version, so it needs no confirmation (L-58). Save and Reload keep the width of the wider of their idle words and their busy words with the spinner before them, so the press that starts a save never moves Discard (L-64), that room is the words' cell, not the button's padding, so Save pads as Discard does and a short busy word adds no room past the idle one's, and busy the spinner stands just before its word, the two centred as one group, and actions that wrap under the bar's copy fill their line as a dialog's do on a phone, in equal halves while each label fits its half, a longer one widening to its words while both still fit one row, and stacked once they do not (L-58). The gold edge of a save bar or a decision callout is one stroke wide (`--sui-decision-border-width`) in the unsaved edge (`--sui-unsaved-border`), darker than the gold fill, so the frame never outshines the gold action it holds, since the gold action already carries the hue and a heavier frame outweighs the title, and a failed or refused save leads its copy with a field error's warning mark, beside its title and reason as a callout's symbol stands beside its words, with the reason at a field error's weight, in the danger ink for a failed save and, for a conflict, which lost no work and waits on the gold Reload, Reload's refresh mark, a shape apart from a failure's warning, in the unsaved ink over a reason in the subtitle's ink and weight, and a conflict's title names Reload, its one way on, so it differs from the subtitle by more than colour (WCAG 1.4.1). `S-36 S-39 S-40` `review` `audit`
- **L-17** No decorative gradients, glows, glass surfaces or left-edge accent strips. A surface carries state with its fill, words and marks, never a colored edge on one side. `S-2 S-41` `audit`
- **L-114** A reader who asks for more contrast (`prefers-contrast: more`, Apple's Increase Contrast) gets lines that hold 3:1 on every ground in both themes: the hairlines and dividers of L-8 and the quiet button and field frames of L-9 take `--sui-border-contrast`, and hover takes `--sui-border-contrast-hover`, a step firmer. Only the lines change, so nothing moves (L-71) and words keep their inks (WCAG 1.4.11). `audit`

## Fonts and type

- **L-18** Two families, the same faces Smyklot ships: `SUI Plus Jakarta Sans` (variable 400 to 700) for interface text and `SUI JetBrains Mono` (variable 400 to 500) for technical values. Each has a Latin and a Latin Extended face with `font-display: swap`. The Latin faces ride inline in the stylesheet, and the kitchen sink holds its paint until they decode, so text is drawn in its own face from the first frame and never swaps. `S-48 K-14 K-15` `source`
- **L-19** Text never asks a face for a weight it does not have: sans stays within 400 to 700 and mono within 400 to 500. `K-21` `audit`
- **L-20** Headings, navigation, labels, buttons and prose are never monospace. Technical values (IDs, paths, versions, code, counts) are mono with tabular figures, and an amount typed beside its unit word, such as a duration's, keeps the sans face with tabular figures, so it reads as one phrase with its unit, and a page translator leaves them as written (`translate="no"`), in the words a screen reader hears too, while the words heard with them, such as a diff line’s “Added”, stay open to translation. A number on a chart, a tick or a cell value, keeps the sans face with tabular figures, so values down an axis line up. `S-49 S-223 K-22` `audit`
- **L-21** Sizes come from `--sui-font-size-*` and leading from `--sui-leading-*` or `--sui-row-copy-leading`. Every `font` shorthand carries its leading. Every leading is a whole pixel at a 16px root, so rendered line boxes never land on half pixels. The one exception is `--sui-row-copy-leading`, the cap height plus the 8px copy gap, which is fractional by design. `S-51 S-52` `source` `audit`
- **L-22** The phone (47.9375rem and narrower) reads body text one step larger with its leading, and the page title steps down from 32px to 24px. Text a reader types is 16px on a phone (`--sui-font-size-input`), because iOS Safari zooms the page into a focused field whose text is smaller. A technical value typed in mono stands half a pixel under the words on a desktop, 13.5px (`--sui-font-size-input-mono`), so its taller x-height matches theirs, since the mono face reads larger at one size, and types at 16px with them on a phone or touch screen. `S-53` `source`
- **L-23** Titles, labels, button labels, pills and chips are trimmed to the cap height and the baseline (`text-box: trim-both cap alphabetic`). A trimmed box never clips its ink. `S-54 S-59 K-19` `audit`
- **L-24** Copy lines stand 8px apart as rendered ink (`--sui-row-copy-gap`): a row's name and its description, and the wrapped lines of one text. A title, a heading or the title of a panel, card, chart or bar, stands 12px above its description (`--sui-title-copy-gap`), and a dialog's 16px (L-58), so the two read as a title and its text rather than one paragraph (shadcn/ui and Radix Themes part a card title from its text by about 12px of ink). A setting row's status under its description, its saving word, locked reason or refusal, stands 12px under it too (right under the name, as the row's only copy line, it stands 8px off), so it reads as a line of its own and not one more line of the description, as a field's error stands a step further from the hint over it (L-35). Form help, form errors, a toast's description, a save bar's subtitle, a dialog's description and a page header's description, the consequence a reader weighs before answering and so set at the body size, a step over its meta notes, wrap at 20px leading (`--sui-leading-note`), 22px on a phone under its 44px controls, so two lines of it never read as pressed under the bold title, a little looser than a row's copy so a long hint stays a quiet note, never body leading, and a toast's description wraps in balanced lines, so the few lines a reader takes in at a glance never end on a stranded word, save beside an action, where balanced lines leave a gap before it and the reason wraps pretty. An identity row's semibold name wraps at that note leading too, so a long name beside its avatar never presses into one block in a narrow column. A card's or a section's description wraps at 20px too (`--sui-leading-title-note`), with no phone step, as no control stands under it, so its 14px lines never press into one block under the title's 24px ones. A page, card or plate title is set tighter than copy (a page title about 1.15 rounded to 4px, Atlassian's 28/32 and 24/28, a card, plate or section title 17/24, so a long title's semibold lines never press into one block), while a save bar's title, a bar's and not a plate's, wraps at the body leading (`--sui-leading-body`, 23px, and 24px on a phone, where it grows to 16px), so four semibold lines of it never press into one block over the lighter note, and its description stands the title's 12px copy gap under it, ink to ink, as a card's does, so the two read as one message. The gap comes from the title's line height, so it holds when a reader raises line height to 1.5 (WCAG 1.4.12). `S-55 S-57` `audit`
- **L-25** The tiers: page title 32px 700, dialog title 20px 700 at -0.015em and a confirmation's question 18px, since a question and its two answers read as one compact block and a 20px question outranks the page around it, card, section and empty or error panel title 17px 600 balanced, 18px on a phone where the body is 16px (a failed refresh over a list keeps 16px), sub-section title 15px 600, the body's 16px on a phone, so it stays a step under its section's title, a popover's title 16px 600 (`--sui-font-size-title`), two steps over its 14px copy, since a short panel has no room to part them by space, body 15px, control and label 14px with labels at 600, while the words beside a switch, a check box or a radio are the option itself, 14px at the regular weight, as GOV.UK, Apple, Material and Radix Themes set them, since a heavier option reads as a label over a control that is not there, and a form's help or hint 14px, the size of the label it explains, quieter by its regular weight and secondary ink, so it reads as the label's note and stays easy to read, with an error under a field at the same size, set apart by its weight and ink (L-35), so a field and its notes keep one size. A section or sub-section title is Title Case and never uppercase. A count beside a label, in a tab, a segment, a section's title or a sidebar row, is caption type, 13px at 600 at every width, a step under the 14px label it counts and on its own veil, so it reads as data and not as a mark, and its figures hold beside a 17px title. No text is smaller than 11px, the Apple minimum, save the initials of an avatar under 24px, a mark the reader never reads as words, hidden from assistive technology beside the name it stands for, set at 9px so two letters keep clear of so small a circle's edge, near the share of the mark that the larger steps' letters take. Eyebrows, group headings and column headings may be uppercase micro or compact type at 600 with 0.04em to 0.07em tracking, for one to three words only, and a picker's group heading is compact type at 0.04em and 500, so it holds its own beside the 14px names it heads without outweighing them. An option in a select's or a combobox's list is the value itself, so it is set as the field sets that value, at the field's size and the regular weight, with its note a type step under it (13px, 14px on a phone where the name is 16px) so the two part by size as well as ink, and a menu item takes the same regular weight, while a tooltip is caption type, 13px at the regular weight, a step under the words of the control it names, as shadcn/ui and Radix Themes set their tips, as shadcn/ui, Radix Themes, Apple and Material set their menus, since a list of commands at a button's 500 reads as a stack of bold buttons, with its description a type step under it (13px under the 14px name) so a disabled item's reason never outranks its name. A table's column headings are control type at 600 in the primary ink and sentence case, a step under the rows they head on a desktop and on a phone, as shadcn/ui and Radix Themes head their tables at 14px. A sortable heading's arrow rests in the muted ink, and the sorted column's arrow wears the accent's text ink, so the column the rows follow reads from across the table. All caps reads about 10% slower (NN/g), and VoiceOver can spell a short uppercase word letter by letter. `S-61 S-62 S-63` `review`
- **L-26** Contents of a centred single-line row are centred by eye: the cap-to-baseline band of text and the border box of painted things share a centre within 0.25px. No `translate` nudges. `S-60` `audit`
- **L-115** Running text wraps at `--sui-measure`, 50ch, about 80 characters of the sans and the line length WCAG 1.4.8 names: a page header's description, a section's note and paragraphs, a card's note, a callout's copy, an empty or error state's explanation, a setting's why line and a link's note. A wide pane never stretches a line past it. Rows, labels and values are not running text and take their room. `review`
- **L-27** A value inside a sentence breaks with it (`overflow-wrap: anywhere`), and an atom such as "84.7 MB" stays on one line: a number and its unit, a size, a time or a span such as "2 days", are joined by a non-breaking space (`marks.unit`, `&nbsp;`), and a counted time from `RelativeTime` keeps its count and unit together. Facts on one line are parted by a drawn dot (L-103). `S-83 S-162` `audit`

## Copy

- **L-28** Labels, hints, descriptions, list items, toasts and single-sentence paragraphs end without a period, as Material and GOV.UK write interface text. A paragraph of two or more sentences ends each sentence with a period. `S-241 K-27` `audit`
- **L-29** A sentence holds at most 25 words, and a paragraph at most 3 sentences. `K-28 K-29` `audit`
- **L-30** Headings and buttons are Chicago Title Case: page, section, card, plate, dialog, chart, empty and error state titles, the words of a button or a link drawn as one, a summary, a tab and a menu item. Inside a title, articles, "and", "but", "for", "or", "nor", "to", "as" and prepositions stay lower case, the first and last words are capitalized, and "&" stands for "and". Code, a name with its own casing and a quoted value keep their case. Labels, hints, tooltips, callouts, toasts and running copy stay sentence case. A button is a verb phrase that names its result. An empty state is a plain title, one explanation and a next step, with no decorative icon. A toast description does not repeat its title. `S-232 S-241 K-36` `audit` `review`
- **L-31** UI copy uses the real ellipsis character "…" and curly quotes (“ ” ‘ ’), never three dots or a straight quote, and a placeholder, a select's prompt too, ends with the ellipsis, since it says the field is still empty. `K-38` `audit`

## Spacing and rhythm

- **L-32** Spacing comes from the 4px scale (`--sui-space-1` to `--sui-space-8`: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32px) and the named rhythm tokens. `S-64 K-43` `source`
- **L-33** A card owns its 20px inset (16px once the card itself is 36rem wide or narrower), and a plate its 16px, and its content stands that inset from all four keylines within half a pixel, measured to the ink a reader sees at rest: the first ink sits the inset below the top, the last the inset above the bottom, and whatever reaches a side stops the inset short of it. Peer cards side by side take their row's height, so they end on one line, and a shorter one holds the extra room under its last ink. Ink is words from cap line to baseline, a glyph's drawn strokes, a placeholder's bar and any box with a frame or ground of its own. A head action centres on the title's cap band and may reach above and below the title's line into the inset (L-34): a framed one ends on the inset, and one with no frame or ground at rest, a ghost button or a quiet icon button, keeps its words or glyph at the inset with its box as far from the end edge as from the top, so its hover ground sits square in the corner. A button, a select or a segmented track there is drawn at the quiet tier's 28px, as a callout's actions are, so it stands no taller than it must beside a 24px title line, and grows only its hit area on a phone (WCAG 2.5.8), as Material draws a 40dp button in a 48dp target, so it never reaches the keyline. The footer's actions stand beside the copy at its foot while the card holds both, and on a line of their own under it otherwise, and keep the inset in either place. A row list or a placeholder that opens or closes the card hands back what its rows stand above their first ink and below their last. Only a row's hover, state or stripe ground, an unframed control's ground and a chart's band (L-66), which reaches the holder's edges through `--sui-local-bleed`, may reach into the inset. Cards are spaced by their container's 16px gap and never by their own margin, and a card never decides its column width (`min-width: 0`).
