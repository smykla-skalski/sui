# SUI design laws

These laws govern every SUI component and the kitchen sink that shows them. They come from two sheets: the [Smyklot panel design system](https://github.com/smykla-skalski/smyklot/blob/main/design-system/smyklot-panel/MASTER.md) (`S-n`) and the rules of a companion documentation site (`K-n`). The palette's grounds, lines, inks, accent and status colors come from a companion operations console. When the sheets disagree, Smyklot wins and the companion sheet fills what Smyklot does not say. Where the Smyklot sheet and its tested `tokens.css` disagree, SUI follows the tested code.

A sheet rule that goes against an accessibility or usability standard does not stand. Where a law departs from its sheet for that reason, it names the standard: WCAG 2.2, the WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices (APG), the Apple and Material platform guidelines, GOV.UK, or published research.

Each law names how it is held:

- `audit` - the kitchen sink measures it in the rendered page (`src/routes/audit/`), and the browser suite (`tests/browser/*.test.mjs`) fails on any finding at 320, 375, 768 and 1440px in both themes, and checks the laws that need a keyboard, a media emulation, a right-to-left page or two renders
- `source` - `tests/laws.test.mjs` reads the component sources and the palette
- `review` - a person checks it, because no measurement decides it

A law that says "every" means every component, including the ones added after it was written. When a new component cannot meet a law, change the law here first.

## Palette and color

- **L-1** The palette (`src/lib/styles.css`) is the only place a color is written. Component styles read `var(--sui-*)` and never a hex, `rgb()`, `hsl()` or named color. A shade derived from a color the reader picked, with relative color syntax (`oklch(from var(…) …)`), is not a written color. `S-7` `source`
- **L-2** Every token a component reads is declared, and every declared token is read, directly or through another token. A scale is declared whole even when one step is unused. `S-8 S-9` `source`
- **L-3** Components read semantic tokens, never `--sui-primitive-*`. `K-1` `source`
- **L-4** A token derived with `var()` is restated in every palette block, so a dark subtree never keeps a light alias. A page that chooses no theme follows the reader's system preference to the same dark block. `S-10` `source`
- **L-5** Text holds 4.5:1 against the ground it is painted on, and large text (24px, or 18.66px at 700) holds 3:1, in both themes. `S-11 S-212` `audit`
- **L-6** The focus ring holds 3:1 on the canvas, surfaces, control fills and input fills. A control whose ring is the control itself (switch track, check box, choice dot) uses `--sui-border-strong`, which holds 3:1. `S-18 S-20` `audit`
- **L-7** Ink on a tone fill comes from its partner token (`--sui-on-brand-action`, `--sui-on-danger`, `--sui-on-decision-accent`), never a literal white. `S-12` `source`
- **L-8** Structural hairlines stay subtle: `--sui-border-subtle`, which frames a surface, is between 1.2:1 and 2:1 on `--sui-surface-base`, and `--sui-border-divider`, which separates rows, heads, feet and tab or chart lines inside a surface, is between 1.1:1 and 1.5:1. `S-13` `audit`
- **L-9** A button's words identify it, so its boundary may stay quiet: `--sui-control-border` on `--sui-control-bg` is between 1.5:1 and 2.25:1, and on `--sui-input-bg` between 1.4:1 and 2.25:1. A field is a quiet frame with a baseline: the frame (`--sui-field-frame`, the control border) shapes it, and the baseline (`--sui-field-border`) is what shows that a field is there, so it holds 3:1 against every ground a field stands on and against the field's own fills, as the minimal input in the WCAG 1.4.11 examples does. An invalid or focused field takes its state's color on all four sides. This applies to text fields, text areas, select triggers and joined fields. On hover a field takes the row veil and its edges step up, an invalid field keeps its danger edge a step deeper (`--sui-danger-hover`), and no field adds an inner shadow at rest, because an inset means pressed (L-42). `S-14` `audit`
- **L-10** A control fill is never a focal surface: `--sui-control-bg` on `--sui-surface-base` stays under 1.5:1. An editable field is a distinct inset: `--sui-input-bg` on `--sui-surface-base` is between 1.05:1 and 1.3:1. A fill never marks a field alone. The edge of L-9 does that. `S-15 S-16` `audit`
- **L-11** Hover is one state change and press is two, in one direction. Measured as CIEDE2000 from the ground: on a light ground hover is 4.4 to 5.4 and press 7.2 to 8.3, on a dark ground hover is 5.3 to 6.7 and press 9 to 10.5. Hover and press are alpha veils over the ground, never flat fills. `S-27 S-30` `audit`
- **L-12** A row's hover step is about half a control's: 2 to 3 on light, 4.5 to 5.5 on dark. `S-31` `audit`
- **L-13** The primary button walks one way along its ramp: rest, hover and press move lightness in one direction, and `--sui-on-brand-action` holds 4.5:1 on all three. Destructive hover deepens on light and brightens on dark. `--sui-brand-action` is one color in both themes, so the accent reads the same on either ground. `S-19 S-29` `audit`
- **L-14** The accent is one ocean blue that fills the same in both themes with white ink, and never a destructive color. Its text shade is a step deeper on light and a light blue on dark, and links and information wear it. Success is green, caution is amber, danger is muted rose. A role wears violet (`--sui-role-tint`, `--sui-role-ink`), never an outcome tint. Each hue has an ink for text and a quiet tint for grounds in each theme, and the ink holds 4.5:1 on its tint (L-5). Lines are black or white veils, so one line reads the same on every ground. Chart series have their own palette (L-66) and never wear these. `S-3 S-4 S-21` `review`
- **L-15** Color never carries state alone. Every state color comes with a word, or with an icon that has a text name. An `aria-hidden` icon tells a screen reader nothing, so the state needs words for it (WCAG 1.3.1). The budget is roughly 85% neutral, 10% accent and at most 5% semantic color. `S-5 S-6` `review`
- **L-16** Unsaved work is a caution, so it wears the caution hue: `--sui-decision-accent` is a gold fill beside the warning amber, with its own dark ink, and never stands as text, where the unsaved ink does. The gold `--sui-unsaved-*` surfaces and inks belong only to unsaved state and the action that resolves it, and they always come with their words or mark. A pane shows unsaved work in two places only: its save bar and the changed row's mark, never in a toast. Inside a changed row every edge, field border and on-state fill takes the unsaved family too, so no neutral line or accent fill sits on the gold plate. `S-36 S-39 S-40` `review` `audit`
- **L-17** No decorative gradients, glows, glass surfaces or left-edge accent strips. A surface carries state with its fill, words and marks, never a colored edge on one side. `S-2 S-41` `audit`

## Fonts and type

- **L-18** Two families, the same faces Smyklot ships: `SUI Plus Jakarta Sans` (variable 400 to 700) for interface text and `SUI JetBrains Mono` (variable 400 to 500) for technical values. Each has a Latin and a Latin Extended face with `font-display: swap`. `S-48 K-14 K-15` `source`
- **L-19** Text never asks a face for a weight it does not have: sans stays within 400 to 700 and mono within 400 to 500. `K-21` `audit`
- **L-20** Headings, navigation, labels, buttons and prose are never monospace. Technical values (IDs, paths, versions, code, counts) are mono with tabular figures. `S-49 S-223 K-22` `audit`
- **L-21** Sizes come from `--sui-font-size-*` and leading from `--sui-leading-*` or `--sui-row-copy-leading`. Every `font` shorthand carries its leading. Every leading is a whole pixel at a 16px root, so rendered line boxes never land on half pixels. The one exception is `--sui-row-copy-leading`, the cap height plus the 8px copy gap, which is fractional by design. `S-51 S-52` `source` `audit`
- **L-22** The phone (47.9375rem and narrower) reads one step larger with its leading, and the page title steps down from 28px to 24px. Text a reader types is 16px on a phone (`--sui-font-size-input`), because iOS Safari zooms the page into a focused field whose text is smaller. `S-53` `source`
- **L-23** Titles, labels, button labels, pills and chips are trimmed to the cap height and the baseline (`text-box: trim-both cap alphabetic`). A trimmed box never clips its ink. `S-54 S-59 K-19` `audit`
- **L-24** A title and its description stand 8px apart as rendered ink (`--sui-row-copy-gap`), and wrapped lines keep the same 8px ink gap. Form help and form errors use the same rhythm, never body leading. `S-55 S-57` `audit`
- **L-25** The tiers: page title 28px 700, dialog title 20px 700 at -0.015em, card title 17px 600 balanced, body 15px, control and label 13px with labels at 600. No text is smaller than 11px, the Apple minimum. Eyebrows, group headings and column headings may be uppercase micro or compact type at 600 with 0.06em to 0.07em tracking, for one to three words only. All caps reads about 10% slower (NN/g), and VoiceOver can spell a short uppercase word letter by letter. `S-61 S-62 S-63` `review`
- **L-26** Contents of a centred single-line row are centred by eye: the cap-to-baseline band of text and the border box of painted things share a centre within 0.25px. No `translate` nudges. `S-60` `audit`
- **L-27** A value inside a sentence breaks with it (`overflow-wrap: anywhere`), and an atom such as "84.7 MB" stays on one line. Facts on one line are parted by a drawn dot (L-103). `S-83 S-162` `audit`

## Copy

- **L-28** Labels, hints, descriptions, list items, toasts and single-sentence paragraphs end without a period, as Material and GOV.UK write interface text. A paragraph of two or more sentences ends each sentence with a period. `S-241 K-27` `audit`
- **L-29** A sentence holds at most 25 words, and a paragraph at most 3 sentences. `K-28 K-29` `audit`
- **L-30** Headings and buttons are sentence case. A button is a verb phrase that names its result. An empty state is a plain title, one explanation and a next step, with no decorative icon. A toast description does not repeat its title. `S-232 S-241 K-36` `review`
- **L-31** UI copy uses the real ellipsis character "…", never three dots. `K-38` `audit`

## Spacing and rhythm

- **L-32** Spacing comes from the 4px scale (`--sui-space-1` to `--sui-space-8`: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32px) and the named rhythm tokens. `S-64 K-43` `source`
- **L-33** A card owns its 20px inset (16px once the card itself is 36rem wide or narrower), and its content stands that inset from all four keylines: the first ink sits the inset below the top, the last the inset above the bottom, and whatever reaches a side stops the inset short of it. A control in the head or the footer sets that row's height instead of bleeding into the inset, and a row list that opens or closes the card hands back its row padding. Only a row's hover or state ground may reach into the inset. Cards are spaced by their container's 16px gap and never by their own margin, and a card never decides its column width (`min-width: 0`). `S-65 S-66 S-67` `audit`
- **L-34** What stands beside a heading does not set the heading row's height: a one-line head row is at most 0.6px taller than its heading. Head controls centre on the title's cap line. A card's head is the exception: it is as tall as its control and the title centres on that, so the control keeps the card's inset (L-33). `S-68 S-69` `audit`
- **L-35** A form stacks fields 16px apart, with 8px between label, control and help. A label and a switch in one row share a centre. A field that must be filled says "Required" in words beside its label, and its control is `required`. `S-74` `audit`
- **L-36** A row pads 12px, its 1px hairline sits in its own band inset by the row padding, and a setting row is at least 44px tall. `S-73` `review`

## Controls

- **L-37** Standalone controls are 34px (`--sui-control-height-compact`) on a desktop and 44px on a phone (47.9375rem and narrower), where a finger needs 44px (Apple HIG, WCAG 2.5.5). A surface may opt into the 40px tier through `--sui-local-control-height`, never through local padding. Every rendered control height is a whole pixel from the set 20, 24, 28, 34, 40, 44. `S-85 S-86` `audit`
- **L-38** Every pointer target is at least 24 by 24px (WCAG 2.5.8). On a phone every target is 44 by 44px: a standalone control by its height, and a small one (a switch, a chip's remove button, a help mark) by a pseudo-element hit area. Two targets that stand apart never reach into each other's hit area. `S-87 S-215 K-125` `audit`
- **L-39** Button tones are jobs: `default` an ordinary action, `signal` the one action a view exists for (one per view), `ghost` low emphasis, `stop` destructive, `stop-quiet` destructive and bordered, `add` an optional addition, `decision` the action that resolves unsaved work. Disabled is a state, not a tone. `S-90` `review`
- **L-40** Disabling is a last resort. An action whose input is missing or wrong stays enabled. When pressed, it says what is missing and moves focus there (GOV.UK, Adrian Roselli). A control is disabled only while it cannot run at all, such as during a save or without permission, and a visible reason stands beside it. A disabled control renders at `--sui-disabled-opacity` (0.7), so its words keep about 3:1, with the default cursor, and it is never hidden. `S-92 K-54` `audit`
- **L-41** Focus draws one 2px `--sui-focus` outline, never a box shadow. A bordered control is flush (offset 0, its border takes the focus color), a free-standing control is offset 2px, and anything inside a clipping container is inset 2px. `S-93 K-124` `audit`
- **L-42** Every control a reader presses shows the press, save text links: its ground takes `--sui-interactive-pressed` with `--sui-pressed-inset`, and a control with a frame of its own also sinks 1px (`translate: 0 1px`), with no scale. A part inside a shared frame, such as a chip's remove part or a segmented option, takes the ground and the inset without the sink. Hover and press never paint at the same time, and a press never selects the control's words: every control a reader presses sets `user-select: none`, while text links and content stay selectable. `S-94` `audit`
- **L-43** A mark is as wide as what it says: buttons, chips, pills, links and switches are `fit-content`. `S-89` `audit`
- **L-44** An icon-only button carries a glyph that every reader reads the same way, such as close, more, search, delete or settings. Any other action shows its word (NN/g). It is square at its tier (34px, or 28px quiet), has an accessible name that doubles as its tooltip, and never takes a text button's padding. `S-96 S-128 S-175` `audit`
- **L-45** The `add` tone (dashed, pill radius, leading plus, specific label) only starts an optional entry. It never submits, saves, cancels, resets, removes, navigates or picks an existing value. Cancel is a bordered default button placed before the primary action, so the primary action sits at the trailing edge (Material, Apple). `S-104 S-106 S-108` `review`
- **L-46** A primitive owns its look. A parent never styles a child primitive's border, ground, type, focus or height, and no component overrides another's classes with `:global`. A component may ask whether another is its child, inside `:has()`, to lay out its own box. `S-109 S-110` `source`
- **L-47** Text fields rest on `--sui-control-bg` inside the field edge of L-9, take `--sui-input-bg` on focus, and are symmetric about their line (top padding plus border equals bottom). A field with a second part, such as a unit or a copy action, is one field: the part sits inside its end with no line before it, and one frame and one error edge cover both, and the ring marks the part that holds focus: the whole field for the value, an inset ring on the end part. `S-112 S-113` `audit`
- **L-48** A picker is a native `<select>`, styled with customizable select (`appearance: base-select`) where the browser has it. Elsewhere the browser's own picker shows, which is the most robust picker on phones and with screen readers (GOV.UK, Sarah Higley). A select holds six or more options. Two to five that fit are a segmented control or radios. The picker is themed, at least as wide as its trigger, inside the viewport, at most `min(20rem, available height)` tall, and every option is at least one control height (34px, or 44px on phones). Option text wraps. Only the trigger may cut its value, because opening it shows the whole value. `S-97 S-98` `audit`
- **L-49** A boolean that takes effect the moment it flips is a `Switch`: a 34 by 20px track with a 16px thumb travelling 14px and a 44px tap box. A boolean that a later save writes, a multi-choice or a consent is a checkbox with a visible 18px box (NN/g, Material). Native checkbox paint never shows. `S-99 S-100` `audit`
- **L-50** A segmented control holds 2 to 5 options. It draws one solid thumb exactly the selected option's width, seated by a small shadow, that does not slide on first paint, travels 240ms between options and draws no thumb for `null`. A hover or press fill keeps the thumb's curve, concentric with the track, at the track's ends, and squares and runs one radius under the thumb where it meets it, so no sliver of track shows at the join. A press sinks the fill into the track, and the thumb darkens and creases without the 1px sink. The thumb can be dragged to another option and chooses it on release, and a click or the arrow keys still reach every option (WCAG 2.5.7). When its options do not fit, it turns into a select, because a hidden option is a lost option. `S-114 S-115 S-116 S-117` `audit`
- **L-51** A combobox or listbox follows the ARIA pattern: arrows move with wrap-around, Enter commits, Escape closes without closing a surrounding dialog, and `aria-activedescendant` is set only once an arrow key has moved to an option. A search in progress and an empty result are announced. `S-120 K-66 K-67` `review`
- **L-52** Opening or closing a popover, menu or inline editor keeps the trigger in place and the page scroll unchanged. Escape closes it and returns focus to the trigger. Tab closes it and lets focus move on (APG menu button). `S-124 S-126` `audit`
- **L-92** A control inside a container that wears a state (a warning or danger tint or frame, an unsaved decision) takes that state's family: its ground, edge and hover come from the state's hue, never a neutral raised block on the state. On a tint the default action is quiet, with no ground of its own and an edge of the tone, as Carbon's ghost action in an inline notification and Atlassian's link actions in a section message are. `audit`
- **L-93** A painted swatch, such as a picked colour or a swatch to pick, holds 3:1 against its ground (WCAG 1.4.11) or wears a ring that does. The ring is the swatch's own colour with its lightness clamped into the theme's readable band, so a colour that already contrasts shows no ring and one that does not gets the nearest shade of itself that does, never a grey line that overpowers it. `audit`

## Surfaces

- **L-53** Radii: check box 5px, chip 6px, control 8px, surface 10px, popover 12px, dialog 14px, pill fully round. Edges, ring shadows and dividers are `--sui-stroke` (1px) or `--sui-stroke-bold` (2px) wide. `S-129` `source`
- **L-54** Shadows belong only to plates (including the selection plate of a sidebar or segmented control), popovers, menus and dialogs. `S-130` `review`
- **L-55** A card is `--sui-surface-base` with a 1px `--sui-border-subtle` keyline and a 10px radius. Surfaces are not nested for decoration: a card, callout or code block never sits inside another framed surface only to frame it. The kitchen sink's example frame is a viewport around live specimens, not a surface of the design, so it may hold surfaces. Each specimen's name and props sit under it, below a dashed rule, and specimens on one line share the tallest one's height so their captions hang from one line. The frame's footer holds the example's tools. A component stays inside its specimen, and a caption line wraps only where the specimen cannot grow wider. A stack is for specimens that use their line; specimens that each fill under 60% of it and fit side by side share a row. `S-131 S-134` `review` `audit`
- **L-56** A message's symbol (a callout's, a failed toast's) stands in a 2rem tile of the message's tone with an 18px glyph. Copy shorter than the tile centres on it within 0.5px, and longer copy starts at the tile's top, so the space above the tile equals the space below the copy. A toast hangs its tile, its title's first line, its action and Dismiss on one first line as tall as the buttons, so the tile centres on the title's cap band within half a pixel. A callout has two tones (`quiet`, `warning`) plus the `decision` surface, and a warning says "Warning" in words that a screen reader reads. `S-135 S-137 S-138` `audit`
- **L-57** A disclosure is a quiet native `<details>` with a trailing chevron inside its hit area. Controls revealed by a disclosure sit in their own card. `S-132 S-139 S-140` `audit`
- **L-58** A dialog is for a task that needs an answer, a form or a decision. Reading belongs on a page or in a panel. A dialog is 34rem wide (inspector 40rem, wide 46rem), over a 56% light or 62% dark scrim with an 8px blur. It closes on Escape and on its close button, and on an outside press only when it holds no field, so a stray click never loses typed work. When closing would discard work, it confirms first. It traps focus, opens on its first field and never on its close button, and returns focus to its trigger. A confirmation is an `alertdialog`, asked only for an act that cannot be undone. An act that can be undone happens at once and offers Undo (NN/g, Apple). `S-147 S-148` `review`
- **L-59** An empty or error state puts its title above its explanation with the 8px copy gap and offers a next step. `S-150 S-233` `audit`
- **L-60** A toast says what just happened. A toast without an action leaves after 5 seconds and pauses while hovered or focused. A toast with an action such as Undo stays until the reader acts or dismisses it, and so does a failure (WCAG 2.2.1, Material). A toast is never the only way to do something, and never shows a lasting state such as unsaved work. Toasts stand above an open dialog, and closing one never drops focus to the page. `S-47` `review`

## Lists and data

- **L-61** Lists are semantic `ul` and `li` rows that grow when their content wraps, with `role="list"` when they drop their bullets, because Safari drops the list otherwise. Lists draw no zebra stripes and no fixed row heights, and the page keeps one scroll. `S-152 S-153 S-158` `audit`
- **L-62** A data table has one heading recipe, aligns numbers at the end with tabular figures, and scrolls inside its own frame. No cell draws outside its column. A wide table may stripe its rows to keep the reader's place (Enders, NN/g). `S-160 S-161 K-93` `audit`
- **L-63** A long list loads its next page from a "Load more" button that keeps focus and announces how many rows arrived. A list never loads on scroll alone, because that hides the footer and traps keyboard readers (NN/g, GOV.UK). A data table that readers search or return to may use numbered pages and a page-size choice instead. `S-155` `review`
- **L-64** Loading reserves the final geometry, keeps loaded rows during a refresh, marks its region `aria-busy`, and every placeholder shares one pulse. `S-156 S-208 S-234` `audit`
- **L-65** Code colors belong to the language, hold 4.5:1 on their ground, and selected code is opaque. Every line of a code or diff block is one height, and the block has no gap above its first line or below its last, so a line's tone runs from edge to edge. A copy control names what it copies, confirms with "Copied" for 1.6 seconds on every copy, and says so when the copy fails. `S-26 S-227 K-96` `audit`
- **L-66** Charts have a title, a description and a table of their values. Series take the categorical palette `--sui-chart-1` to `--sui-chart-5` in order, never a status color, and every mark holds 3:1 against its surface. Series differ by more than color: each has its own marker shape, each line after the first has its own dash, and up to four lines carry direct labels (WCAG 1.4.1). The form fits the data: a series over time is a line, and two measures of each item are a scatter. `S-222` `audit`
- **L-91** A row's state (a change not saved yet, a refusal, a choice) is a plate drawn just inside the row's own box, never a fill of the whole row. A change of state never moves a row, changes its height or shifts its controls: a mark or control the state brings, such as the unsaved word mark or a reset, keeps its seat while hidden. Two neighbouring plates show a strip of card between them, at least 4px and no more than 8px, so they read as two rows of one list rather than as cards. `audit`
- **L-94** An icon or mark beside words centres on the cap band of their first line within half a pixel, the most whole pixels allow: in a row, a cell, a chip, a menu item or an option, and never on the middle of a block that wraps, as Material's lists top-align a leading icon with the primary text. The words run cap to baseline in a box snapped to an even height, and the mark centres on that one-line box, so both land on whole pixels. A one-line item keeps the control height exactly. A message's symbol in its tile follows L-56 instead. `audit`

## Icons

- **L-67** One outline family on a 24-unit grid with a 1.75 stroke and round caps, from a closed name list. Every glyph is `aria-hidden`, so the words beside it carry its meaning. `S-171 K-133` `audit`
- **L-68** Icon sizes are the tiers 10, 12, 13, 16 and 18px at the default text size (`--sui-icon-*`), and they grow with the reader's text size, rounded to the pixel. `S-52 S-173` `source`
- **L-69** Every glyph's ink is centred in its 24-unit box within 0.25 units. `S-174` `source`

## Layout

- **L-70** No page scrolls sideways at 320px or 375px. `S-185` `audit`
- **L-71** Light and dark share geometry: switching themes moves nothing. `S-184` `audit`
- **L-72** Floating content (dialogs, popovers, menus, tooltips and toasts) lives in the browser's top layer, where `z-index` does nothing and the last one opened is on top. The `--sui-layer-*` tokens order only in-flow chrome, such as sticky bars and the shell, and the parts of a control that isolates its own stacking (`--sui-layer-inner-*`). `S-195` `source`
- **L-90** A row that wraps starts every new line at its start edge. Spread a row with `justify-content: space-between`, which ends a shared line with its last item and starts a line that item takes alone, never with an auto margin that leaves a wrapped item adrift at the far end. A row aligned to its end or centre on purpose keeps every line there. `audit`
- **L-95** Every box lands on whole pixels down the page, so every edge under it draws crisp. A trimmed line rounds its block up to the pixel (`--sui-block-snap`), and any size, padding or margin derived from a cap height or from halving a difference is rounded to the pixel. A fraction is never born in a box's height. A box centred in another takes the other's parity (`--sui-block-snap-even` against the even control heights and floors), so the space either side halves to whole pixels and no border, outline or ground draws on a half pixel. `audit`
- **L-96** Every edge a box draws shows in full, in every state: its border, an outline, a ring shadow and the focus ring. A container that clips leaves room for what its children draw, or its children draw inside themselves (L-41), so no ring is cut at a container's edge. `audit`

## Motion

- **L-73** Every duration and easing reads a token: press 100ms, fast 160ms, normal 220ms, travel 240ms. `S-200` `source`
- **L-74** Hover, press and release are one continuous change. Movement uses transform and opacity, one interaction animates at most two elements, and exit is faster than entry. `S-201 S-203 S-204 S-205` `review`

## Shell

## Source

- **L-82** Components use Svelte 5 runes: no `on:` directives, `export let`, `$$props`, `<slot>`, `$:` or `svelte/store`. `K-131` `source`
- **L-83** Every exported component opens with an `@component` contract and appears in the kitchen sink. - **L-98** Hover paint applies only where the pointer can hover (`@media (hover: hover)`), so a tap on a phone never leaves a control looking hovered. Press, focus and selection paint everywhere. `source`
- **L-99** The layout changes only at the palette's breakpoints, `--sui-breakpoint-phone` (47.9375rem) and `--sui-breakpoint-wide` (64.0625rem). A component that answers to its own width uses a container query at a width the palette names (`--sui-breakpoint-card`, `-row`, `-callout`, `-bar`). A floating bar that would cover most of a short screen, as at 400% zoom, sits in the flow under `(max-height: 30rem)`. `source`
- **L-100** Whatever a click presses or flips shows the pointer cursor before the press: buttons, links, summaries, menu items, options, tabs, and the words of a label that flips a check box, a radio or a switch. - **L-102** Words in a small box of their own ground or frame sit on its middle: the room above the cap line equals the room under the baseline within half a pixel, in a count, a pill, a chip, a tab or a button. - **L-103** Facts on one line are parted by a drawn middle dot (`FactLine`), muted, standing `--sui-sep-space` (9px) from each fact, and nothing beside it adds room: no typed space and no gap.
